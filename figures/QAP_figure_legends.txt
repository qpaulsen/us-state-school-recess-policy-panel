Dataset construction and use. a, The 7 construction stages from source identification to release (grey, construction; blue, quality assurance; green, release), with the codebook version adopted at each stage and applied to all jurisdictions (reliability statistics for round 1 exclude Alabama, the rule-development state); the bracket marks the audit log that records every source, search and decision. b, Research applications of the released data and its linkage to outcome data by state FIPS code and school year.

Figure 2. How instrument rows encode policy change. a, Four successive versions of Mississippi's kindergarten daily activity rule. Bars run from the effective date (diamond), or enactment date (circle) for the 1998 rule, to the amended date; triangles mark the start of the assigned treatment school year, with dashed lines showing the lag from effective date (rule 23). The 1998 rule was in force at panel entry and has no treatment year (left-censored). Bar color shows outdoor specificity. Row identifiers: MS_PA_1998_K_40DAILY, MS_PA_2012_K_30DAILY, MS_PA_2016_K_30DAILY_OUTDOOR, MS_PA_2018_K_30DAILY_OUTDOOR. b, Arkansas recess: a silent baseline (AR_2000BASE_RECESS_NONE) is superseded by Act 641 of 2019 (AR_ACT641_2019_RECESS, a mandate coded outdoors if feasible, effective 2019-07-24); the baseline's amended date equals the act's effective date (rule 26), and the dated rows expand into one cell per school year.

Figure 3. Rollout of state K-12 recess and activity mandates, 2000-2001 to 2025-2026. a, Jurisdictions (50 states and the District of Columbia) with a PE or daily activity time mandate, a recess mandate, or a mandate coded outdoors if feasible in force in each school year; the last series overlaps the other 2, and arrows mark instruments still in force at the end of the window. b,c, Strongest K-12 instrument in force for recess (b) and PE and daily physical activity time (c) in 4 school years. Status is derived from time-dose and structural-recess rows using treatment school year, amended date and repeal date; Pre-K rows are excluded, rows in force at panel entry are shown from 2000-2001, and 8 rows without a treatment year (6 Hawaii, 2 New Mexico) are omitted. DC is shown as a square. Boundaries: US Census Bureau cartographic files (Albers USA projection).

Figure 4. Technical validation. a, Inter-coder agreement by field in the calibration round (Alaska, Arizona, Arkansas; 14 dually detected instruments) and the production round (6 randomly drawn states; 27 matched instruments). Filled points are Cohen's kappa; open points are proportions in exact agreement, used where kappa is undefined (required minutes, numeric; recess withholding in the calibration round, a single category). The dashed line marks 0.8. Frequency and grade band are shown for the calibration round only: in the production round the coders split multi-grade instruments into rows differently, so these 2 fields compare row structure rather than coding (kappa 0.68 and 0.74 on the 34 instruments matched after the convention was applied). Asterisk: the coders applied different conventions for when a requirement counts as monitored; the convention was fixed in codebook version 1.2 (rules 11 and 25) and applied across the panel. b, The 382 candidate instruments raised by the completeness audit, by the check that raised them and by what each did to the panel at the data freeze: a new row written, a coded field of an existing row revised, a note added to an existing row, or closed with no change. Two or more checks: raised independently by more than 1 of the 4 checks. Later review: raised in logged searches outside the 4 numbered checks, including follow-up retrievals and the District of Columbia session.
